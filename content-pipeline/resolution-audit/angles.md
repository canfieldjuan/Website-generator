# Resolution Audit Angles

Use these as starting points for Open WebUI. Keep the claims guard open while
drafting.

## Angle 1: The Ownership Question

Best for Reddit, LinkedIn comments, and low-pitch feedback posts.

Purpose:

- Learn how support and CX teams route repeated questions.
- Surface the gap between "support answered it" and "the root issue ended."

Safe prompts:

- "When the same question hits the support queue hundreds of times a month,
  whose job is it to fix the root: support, product, policy, or nobody?"
- "Do you track which repeated tickets are documentation problems versus
  product or billing problems?"
- "What share of repeated tickets are not really support problems, and how do
  you surface that today?"

CTA posture:

- Ask for replies.
- Do not ask for a CSV in the first post unless the community allows explicit
  self-promotion.

## Angle 2: The Invisible Repeat Cost

Best for LinkedIn POV posts.

Purpose:

- Explain why cheap repeated resolutions can still hide expensive root causes.
- Reframe deflection metrics as incomplete when they do not show what keeps
  recurring.

Safe prompts:

- "A resolved ticket and a fixed problem are not the same thing."
- "Deflection metrics can tell you what did not reach a human. They do not
  always tell you what kept breaking."
- "If the same issue gets resolved over and over, the question is not only
  whether support handled it. The question is why the issue still exists."

CTA posture:

- Ask whether the framing matches what support leaders see.
- Invite examples without requesting private details.

## Angle 3: Build In Public - Report Shape

Best for LinkedIn and founder/operator communities.

Purpose:

- Get feedback on the reframe from "FAQ list" to "audit trail and action
  queue."

Safe prompts:

- "I changed the report shape from 'what should we publish?' to 'what is
  repeating, what evidence exists, and what needs a different owner?' Which
  version would make you act?"
- "Old frame: rank the repeat questions. New frame: show the repeat, the cost
  exposure, the source evidence, and whether there is a proven answer. More
  useful, or too much?"
- "If the report says 'no proven answer,' would that help route the issue, or
  would it feel like the report failed?"

CTA posture:

- Ask for feedback.
- Be transparent that it is your tool.

## Angle 4: Free Snapshot Ask

Best for LinkedIn, direct replies, and communities that allow offers.

Purpose:

- Collect qualified CSV exports for Snapshot runs.
- Keep the ask bounded and honest.

Safe prompts:

- "I am testing a free Resolution Snapshot for support-heavy teams. You upload
  a closed-ticket CSV. I send back the top repeat pattern, estimated cost
  exposure, one evidence-backed answer if the tickets contain one, and one gap
  if they do not."
- "If the export is thin, I will say so. If it has enough repeated-question
  signal, the Snapshot should show whether a full audit is worth reviewing."
- "No guaranteed savings pitch. The deliverable is an audit trail: repeated
  questions, source evidence, review-ready drafts where evidence exists, and
  operational gaps where it does not."

CTA posture:

- Ask for a CSV only after the audience understands the bounded deliverable.
- Offer the upload link or ask them to reply with "audit."

## Angle 5: Pattern Or Finding

Best for LinkedIn and careful Reddit posts.

Purpose:

- Share anonymized lessons without exposing raw data.

Safe prompts:

- "A pattern I am testing: the repeat questions support can answer are not
  always the most expensive ones. The expensive ones often need a product,
  policy, or process fix."
- "The useful finding is not always 'write this help article.' Sometimes it is
  'do not write another help article until the workflow is fixed.'"
- "A good audit should be willing to say 'no proven answer.' Otherwise it is
  just inventing content to make the report look complete."

CTA posture:

- Ask whether the pattern matches the reader's support queue.
- Do not imply this pattern is universal.

## Angle 6: Diagnostic, Not Dashboard

Best for thought-leadership posts and blog intros.

Purpose:

- Explain why the report is an action queue, not another analytics view.

Safe prompts:

- "A dashboard says the topic repeated. A diagnostic says whether there is a
  proven answer, what it costs to keep answering it, and whether documentation
  can actually carry the fix."
- "The point is not another chart. The point is a ranked queue your support,
  content, product, and policy owners can review."
- "Some findings become help-center drafts. Some become operational blind
  spots. The distinction is the value."

CTA posture:

- Invite readers to compare this with how they review repeat tickets today.

## Reply Flow: Public Context First, CSV Later

Use this when someone replies "audit," "yes," or "curious."

The useful flow from the older reply asset:

1. Start with public context, not their private tickets.
2. Look at what can be seen publicly, such as help-center structure, category,
   product type, or visible support language.
3. Offer a rough hypothesis without pretending it is their real ticket data.
4. Ask for a CSV only after explaining that the export is what turns the rough
   hypothesis into a real Snapshot.

Safe reply template:

```text
Thanks, [first name]. I can start with a rough read from public context, but I
would not call that an audit yet.

From the outside, I would look for repeated questions around [theme 1],
[theme 2], and [theme 3]. That is only a hypothesis. The real Snapshot needs
your closed-ticket export because the useful part is the evidence: how often
the question repeats, whether agents already have a proven answer, and whether
the issue looks like documentation or an operational gap.

If you want to test it, upload the CSV here: [link].

If the signal is thin, I will say so. If it is strong, the Snapshot should show
whether a full audit is worth reviewing.
```

Do not add fixed percentages, avoided hires, live deflection rates, or
self-service-center promises to this reply.
